# Editorial Rubric

## Must do

- Stay spoiler-safe.
- Explain why the movie connects, not what happens.
- Sound familiar, casual, and warm, like the friend in the group who already knows how to pitch the movie.
- Help a group decide whether the movie fits tonight.
- Prefer concrete language over criticism filler.
- Keep quoted fragments short, readable in-app, and traceable to stored provenance.
- If the brief asks for quote-backed copy, prefer spoiler-safe Letterboxd excerpts when available.

## Must avoid

- Plot summary as the main move.
- Endings, twists, identity reveals, surprise cameos, or late-film pivots.
- Empty praise like `great performances` without context.
- Review-bomb language, false authority, or universal claims.
- Snark aimed at the movie or the user.
- Long unattributed quote dumps.
- Copy that sounds polished in a critic-y or overly written way.

## Good signals

- Mood and room energy.
- Performance texture.
- Pacing and intensity.
- Craft that changes the viewing experience.
- Audience fit and divisiveness.

## Review pass

Reject or rewrite entries that:

- could spoil a first watch
- sound interchangeable with any other movie
- read like critic copy instead of a movie-night recommendation
- over-explain instead of helping a fast decision
- contradict the bundled metadata
